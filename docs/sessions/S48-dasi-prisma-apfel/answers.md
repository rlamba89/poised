# S48 answers (Rahul, 6 Nov 2026, asked at the start of `/build-session S48`)

All four are the recommended options.

1. **Licences: build all three now; the licences are go-live items.**
   - DASI's exclusive licensor is Greenlight Health (formerly Pattern Health, for Dr Mark Hlatky, who holds the copyright). Its licences are "based on use case", and no fee is published.
   - PRISMA-7 is © CESS (Centre d'expertise en santé de Sherbrooke), and an electronic version seems to need its permission.
   - Apfel is a published risk score, with no licence found.
   - They're built and tested locally with made-up data, as STOP-Bang was. "DASI licence from Greenlight" and "PRISMA-7 permission from CESS" join the go-live list.
2. **DASI: the score, the METs, and one band, "Poor functional capacity", at 34 or less** (Wijeysundera et al., BJA 2020;124(3):261–70: a DASI over 34 had fewer complications). A clinician checks it before go-live.
3. **Both as assessments and as ready-made scores an author can insert in an HQ,** from one definition, as STOP-Bang is.
4. **Apfel in an HQ is the patient's three items only, with no score of its own.** Two questions (smoking; sickness after an anaesthetic or travel sickness), plus the sex from the profile. Otherwise the score would be Incomplete at every send and hold auto-triage (S42). The Apfel assessment on the episode picks them up and waits only for the clinician's post-op opioids item, then gives the score.

**Asked because:** the brief's two questions (1 → our 2, 2 → our 3), the brief's "stop and ask" on licences (our 1), and the S42 consequence of a clinician-only item in an HQ score (our 4).
